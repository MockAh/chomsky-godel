§ 1Goal

Go from watching the machine to giving it named orders. C puts a name on what Phase 1 did by hand: shelves become pointers, the slots on the bench become fixed-size variables and the pile of sheets becomes the function call.

Exit criterion
Write it and see underneath

Writes small C programs that manipulate memory manually, and understands what happens underneath: for a given fragment, says roughly what Assembly it produces and what happens on the stack or the heap.

§ 2How the work is done

  • Desktop WSL to write, compile and debug (gcc, gdb).
  • Phone concept capsules and traces.
  • Format worked example → equivalent exercise done alone. Always predict first.
  • Anchor every C topic is looked at in its Assembly: «what sheet did the compiler write?».

§ 3Challenges

  • Weekly 30–45 min: a short program or a C ↔ Assembly trace.
  • Fortnightly rewrite your mt5.asm in C (adding up an array with a pointer) and compare the compiler's Assembly with yours.
  • Final written when topic 7 is reached. Idea: a linked list on the heap with no leaks, plus a controlled overflow observed in gdb.

Mathematical anchors A type is a finite ring with an interpretation. A pointer is an element of the domain of f : D → {0,1}⁸. A struct is a product with coordinates at fixed positions. free shrinks the domain of a partial function that the program keeps evaluating.

§ 4What to watch out for

  • Shelf vs. piece address versus contents. In mini-task 5 you added to the piece instead of to the shelf; the real addresses in hex got you unstuck. It is the heart of pointers (topic 3).
  • Finiteness type sizes and overflow (topic 2).
  • SF and CF explained, with no exercise of your own yet (topic 2).
  • ZF is not «they are equal»: it is «the last calculation gave 0».
  • The hardware does not recover by itself comes back in manual memory and overflows (topics 5 and 7).
Phase 1's exit door
The neighbouring shelf is not yours

In your mt5, after the four pieces there was a 2c that did not belong to the array. The machine does not know where an array ends: in C that is called a buffer overflow.
