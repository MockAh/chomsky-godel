The chain of the phase From the abstract algebra over {0,1} to the gates that embody it; from a single operation —NAND— to all of arithmetic and all representation of information; and from there to word-sized registers and byte-addressed memory. All of it held up by billions of switches.

§ 1.1Three operations on {0,1}

  • AND (·) 1 only when both operands are 1.
  • OR (+) 1 when at least one is 1.
  • NOT (¬) inverts its single operand.

All computation —an addition, a web page— is, ultimately, a composition of these three.

It is not an analogy
The minimal case of 𝒫(X)

{0,1} with AND, OR, NOT is literally a complemented distributive lattice (Birkhoff). The prototypical example of a Boolean algebra is 𝒫(X) with ∩, ∪ and complement; {0,1} is the minimal case of the same object.

§ 1.2The axioms

  • Idempotence x · x = x; x + x = x
  • Commutativity and associativity of both.
  • Mutual distributivity x·(y+z) = x·y + x·z and also x + y·z = (x+y)·(x+z). Both distributing over each other has no analogue in ℝ.
  • Complement x · ¬x = 0; x + ¬x = 1
  • De Morgan ¬(x·y) = ¬x + ¬y; ¬(x+y) = ¬x · ¬y

§ 1.3Derived operations

XOR and implication are not primitive: they are built.

XOR(x, y) = (x · ¬y) + (¬x · y) x → y = ¬(x · ¬y) = ¬x + y

The second equality can be proved in two ways: by truth table (four cases) or without tables, using only De Morgan and 1 + A = 1. Both are in exercises 2 and 3 of the «Exercises» tab.

§ 2.1The function space

A Boolean function of n variables is f : {0,1}ⁿ → {0,1}. The domain has 2ⁿ elements and each one is assigned 0 or 1:

#functions of n variables = 2^(2ⁿ)

n = 1: 4 functions · n = 2: 16 · n = 3: 256 · n = 4: 65 536.

Notation correction You wrote n^(2²); it is 2^(2ⁿ). Check: n = 2 → 2^(2²) = 2⁴ = 16.

The union over all n is countably infinite: a countable union of finite sets (which calls for a weak form of Choice, Countable Choice).

Many expressions, one function

«What you would do is partition the combinations of NANDs into equivalence classes; that set of classes is the one with 2^(2ⁿ) elements. Right?»
Yes. The count 2^(2ⁿ) is pure combinatorics of the domain and codomain. NAND generates each of those functions, and the relation expression → function is many-to-one. Expressions are the language; functions, the semantics: it is the syntax / semantics distinction of formal logic.

§ 2.2Complete sets

A set of operators is functionally complete if every Boolean function of finite arity can be expressed by composing them.

  • {AND, OR, NOT} the classic basis.
  • {NAND} · {NOR} minimal bases: a single operator.
  • {AND, NOT} · {OR, NOT} the missing one comes from De Morgan.
  • {XOR, AND} less obvious, but complete.

§ 2.3The obstacle: monotonicity

A function is monotone if its output never drops when an input rises from 0 to 1: if x ≤ x' componentwise, f(x) ≤ f(x'). AND and OR are. NOT is not; neither is NAND: NAND(0,1) = 1 but NAND(1,1) = 0.

Why {AND, OR} is not enough
Monotone functions are closed under composition

So no purely monotone set generates NOT. A complete basis needs at least one operator that is non-monotone and non-constant. NAND and NOR are; that is why each one alone is enough.

NOT(x) = NAND(x, x) AND(x, y) = NAND(NAND(x, y), NAND(x, y)) OR(x, y) = NAND(NAND(x, x), NAND(y, y)) NAND generates {NOT, AND, OR}, which is complete: NAND inherits the completeness

§ 2.4Did Turing prove it?

Your question: «Turing proved all this, right?» No. The characterization of which sets of operators are complete is Post's Theorem (Emil Post, 1941). Turing proved something else, at another layer: that there is a universal machine able to simulate any computable procedure. Post says which operations suffice at the logical level; Turing, which procedures are computable. Complementary, but independent.

What it means in the machine: every piece of data is a finite string of bits, every computation is a function {0,1}ⁿ → {0,1}ᵐ decomposable into Boolean functions, and NAND generates them all.

§ 3.1Voltages as bits

The algebra stops being abstract when a high voltage (~3.3 V or ~5 V) is 1 and a low one (~0 V) is 0. A gate is the physical realization of an operation: its electrical behaviour reproduces the truth table.

§ 3.2The faucet metaphor

A transistor is a faucet whose handle is not turned by hand but by another flow: if water reaches its control, the main faucet opens; if not, it stays closed. Three terminals: one for control and two through which the current flows.

  • In series two faucets on the same pipe: water gets through only if both are open. AND.
  • In parallel two pipes that flow into one: one open is enough. OR.
  • Inverted a faucet that conducts when it does not receive a signal. NOT.
De Morgan, wired Series ↔ parallel is the physical version of AND ↔ OR. The algebraic laws show up in the topology of the circuit.

§ 3.3NAND, silicon's favourite

Your first NAND construction The number was right, 3 transistors, but you put the NOT at the start. That inverts a single input and gives ¬A · B. NAND is «the full AND, negated»: first the chain computes A · B and then whatever comes out is inverted.

In real CMOS, NAND is built directly with 4 transistors, without making an AND and negating it; in fact AND is implemented as NAND followed by NOT. NAND is not only complete: it is the cheapest gate to manufacture.

Your NOR, by both routes

Three transistors: two in parallel and a NOT at the end. It is the mirror of NAND.

Four by De Morgan: ¬(x+y) = ¬x · ¬y, two NOTs and an AND in series. Also correct, with one extra transistor.

What you discovered there: algebraically equivalent expressions cost different amounts in silicon. That is what logic minimization is about.

§ 3.4Scale

Tens of billions of transistors on a chip the size of a fingernail, each one opening and closing billions of times per second. A computer does not think: it has trillions of tiny faucets in patterns so complex they look like thought.

§ 4.1One polynomial, many alphabets

N = dₖ·bᵏ + ⋯ + d₁·b¹ + d₀·b⁰

The structure is the same in every base; what changes is the alphabet and the base: {0,1}, {0..7}, {0..9}, {0..9, A..F}. Structurally, positional representation is a homomorphism between strings of symbols and natural numbers.

§ 4.2Converting without arithmetic

The key fact
If b₁ = b₂ᵏ, converting is concatenating

Hex ↔ binary because 16 = 2⁴: 4 bits per digit. Octal ↔ binary because 8 = 2³: 3 bits per digit.

The reason: the weights line up. When a hex digit is multiplied by 16ᵏ = 2^(4k), its 4 bits occupy only the band from 2^(4k) to 2^(4k+3), without overlapping the next digit's band; the sum is then, bit by bit, a concatenation.

Why hex is the language of bits It is not «just another base»: it is binary regrouped into blocks of 4, readable by humans. A byte is exactly two hex digits; that is why addresses and memory dumps are written in hex (you used it throughout the Phase 1 challenge).

§ 5.1Binary addition and the half adder

0 + 0 = 0 0 + 1 = 1 1 + 1 = 10 zero, carry 1 1 + 1 + 1 = 11 one, carry 1

The smallest circuit that adds two bits, the half adder, uses two gates: an XOR gives the sum bit and an AND gives the carry. By chaining full adders (which also accept an incoming carry) a whole word can be added: it is the heart of the ALU.

§ 5.2Subtracting is adding

Computers have no subtraction circuits: A − B ≡ A + (−B), and −B is obtained by two's complement: invert all the bits and add 1. It is a homomorphism between negation in ℤ and a bitwise operation.

§ 5.3Range and sign bit

−2ⁿ⁻¹ ≤ x ≤ 2ⁿ⁻¹ − 1

8 bits: −128 to 127. 64 bits: −2⁶³ to 2⁶³ − 1.

Your question: «Shouldn't it be −2³¹ to 2³¹ − 1 in 64 bits?»
No: one bit is spent on the sign, not half of the bits. The other n − 1 are full magnitude.
Your reconstruction «You only need the last bit, out of the 8, to flip things around. 1000 0000 is such that its two's complement is 0111 1111 + 1 = 1000 0000. That last slot works like the hinge that gives you the whole range between −2⁷ and 2⁷ − 1: 1 sign bit + 7 of magnitude. That is why you don't split the 8 in two; it is the full 7.»
A detail from that same note There you wrote «if it starts with 1 it is positive and if it starts with 0 it is negative». It is the other way round: sign bit 0 → positive, 1 → negative (that is why 1000 0000 is −128).

§ 5.4The asymmetry and its fixed point

There is one more negative than positives: zero takes a place on the positive side and «−0» does not exist. Consequence: the minimum is its own two's complement.

1000 0000 → invert 0111 1111 → +1 → 1000 0000
In your language
Negation is not involutive here

«Negating» has a non-trivial fixed point on the representable set: it breaks the involution and the group structure. There is no +128 in 8 bits. (The same finiteness that reappeared in Phase 1 with 300 → 44.)

§ 6.1Text is a number, by convention

Characters are encoded as natural numbers. The letter 'A' is not 65: it is encoded as 65. It is a lookup table fixed by standards, not a mathematical law.

  • ASCII 7 bits, 128 characters: English, digits, punctuation and control characters (newline, end of transmission). No ñ, no 漢字, no emoji.
  • Unicode a unique natural number for every symbol of every human writing system: more than 150 000. Each one is a code point, U+XXXX in hex. A = U+0041, ñ = U+00F1, π = U+03C0, 漢 = U+6F22, 🔥 = U+1F525.

§ 6.2Code point versus encoding

Two composed functions
symbol → code point → bytes

The first is injective from symbols to ℕ (the what, abstract). The second decides how that number is stored in memory (the how, concrete). There are several possible second functions.

§ 7.1Bit, byte, word

  • Bit an element of {0,1}.
  • Byte 8 bits, by historical convention: 2⁸ = 256 states.
  • Word the processor's natural width: 64 bits = 8 bytes.

The hierarchy is not algebraic: it is a packing convention, with real consequences.

§ 7.2The word defines two things

  • Operands the maximum size the CPU processes in one instruction (the width of its registers).
  • Addresses the size of pointers: 2⁶⁴ ≈ 1.6 × 10¹⁹ addresses, about 16 exabytes.

The jump from 32 to 64 bits came when the 4 GB addressable with 32 bits fell short.

Your question: «Does the word vary from 1 to 8 bytes?»
No: the architectural word is fixed, 8 bytes on 64 bits. What exists are instructions that handle a portion (1, 2 or 4 bytes) and SIMD ones that operate on 128, 256 or 512 bits at once. The word is the reference width, not the only one.

§ 7.3Byte addressing

Each address points to a 1-byte cell, even though the address itself is 8 bytes long. The asymmetry (big pointer, small cell) is intentional: data comes in 1, 2, 4 or 8 bytes, and addressing to the byte lets you touch any of them without masking or shifting. The «waste» of 16 exabytes is nominal.

§ 7.4Atomicity

An operation is atomic if the CPU executes it in a single instruction. That requires two independent conditions:

  1. Size: each operand fits in a word. Adding two 128-bit integers on a 64-bit CPU takes several instructions, propagating the carry by hand.
  2. Arity: arithmetic operations are binary. Adding three numbers is not atomic even if each one fits: it is two chained ADDs.
Why the ALU is binary Its physical adder has two word-wide inputs and one output (plus the carry). To add three you have to go through it twice. That ALU is the box that Phase 1 opened.
